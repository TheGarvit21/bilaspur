Raja Bir Chand established the state of Kehloor in AD 697 (Vikram Samvant 754) with the first capital of his state at Jandbari near Anandpur Sahib. The court poet of Bilaspur, Ganesh Singh Bedi,during his researches for the writing of Chander Vamsh Vilas (1882) and

Shashi Vamsh Vinod 1882, worked out this date based on the available

records with the then ruler, Raja Hira Chand. The Tawarikh-i-Rajputan

Mulk-i-Panjab by Thakur Kahn Singh Balauria also contains the history of

the state. According to the accounts given in these books, Bir Chand

brought under his rule the entire region of Hathawat which, in those

days, was infested with elephants and wild beasts. The lawlessness created

by dacoits and local aggressors was brought under control and peacе

established in the area. One day, a local Gujar by the name of Naina

appeared at the durbar of Raja Bir Chand and informed him that his cow,

even before giving birth to a calf, had been visiting a nearby summit and

yielding milk on a stone-slab. When the Raja visited the spot and had

the slab removed, an idol of goddess Durga was found. The Raja then

had a temple built at the summit (altitude 3,595 feet above sea level)

and named it Naina Devi temple. On another occasion, a Gujar, whose

name was Kehlu, reported to the Raja that his goat, while standing near

the rock of Naina Devi idol, was attacked by a panther thrice but every

time the goat repulsed the attack of the ferocious beast with her horns.

Guided by such propitious signs, Bir Chand realized the spiritual force

of Naina Devi. He shifted his capital from Jandbari to a place later known

as Kot-Kehloor located at the foothill of Naina Devi hill. The ruins of

palaces and other buildings still exist. Presently, the fortress houses a

police station.

The new ruler soon established harmony and peace in overwhelming

force in this newly formed state. Banditry was suppressed. As per the

guidelines in the old Hindu scriptures, the subjects deposited one-sixth

of the produce in the royal treasury in the form of tax. They rejoiced

and lived happily. People of the neighbouring states started approaching

Raja Bir Chand for relief whenever their rulers resorted to cruel rule over

their subjects. It so happened that a Rhund tribe had created lawlessness

in the neighbouring states of Kangra and Suket. The people of those

regions petitioned before the Kehloor Raja for help. Bir Chand attacked

the chief of Kotal caste across river Sutlej in AD 701 and captured

Kotdhar from him. In 704, he invaded both the states of Kangra and

Suket and won battle after battle. He annexed Kangra territories of

Sunhani, Bari and Hatwar. Likewise, he also merged with his state the

Suket hills of Tiun and Sriun. A rule of law and peace was established

in these areas. All arrears of wages of the former state servants were paid.

In 711, the Kehloor Raja wrested five parganas from Sandha and Bandha

(Rhund chiefs of the area). Sandha was the owner of Sandhu Maidan and

Bandha owned Bandla Dhar and lived at Bandlian, a place at old town

of Bilaspur. Sandha and Bandha surrendered before the Raja who was so

pleased with them that they were given the privilege of being the first

ones to make nazars to the Raja during sair durbar. This tradition continued

until 1948. Bir Chand then diverted his attention towards the other hill

states in the neighbourhood. Sirmaur was invaded and brought under

control. Other local chiefs were attacked in 714. Barah Thakurain of

Baghal, Kunihar, Beja, Dhami, Keonthal, Kothar, Jubbal, Baghat, Bhajji,

Mehlog, Mangal and Balson were made tributaries. They were put under

obligation to render feudal service whenever summoned and to attend

for the presentation of their nazars at the yearly sair (last day of the rainy

season) celebration in the month of Ashwani (mid-September). These

thakurain (principalities) assisted in the campaigns against Baddi, Ramsheht,

Chamba, Nalagarh, Plassi, Nawanagar, and Gullarwala. All these tiny

states were annexed and Handur Fort was built to administer these

territories. A court was set up to adjudicate disputes of the subjects and

also the mutual rivalries of the Thakur chiefs.

Alarmed at the growing power of Kehloor, the Sirmaur Raja sought

help from the rulers of Garhwal and Rampur Bushehr to invade Kehloor.
