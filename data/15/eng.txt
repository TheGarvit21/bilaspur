Bhim Chand was fourteen years old when he ascended the throne  in 1665. A council of ministers was set up to run the administration. The ministers in the council included Kasi of Darol, Mian Abhai Ram (ancestor of Bhadrog family) and Parmanand alias Pamma Nadda. Mian Manak Chand (Dip Chand's uncle) headed the council as wazir, but he proved tyrannical and imprisoned the old officials. On hearing this, Jalaldevi, the queen mother (daughter of Raja Sidh Sen of Mandi) had him expelled from the state with the help of Raja Mandi and released the imprisoned officials. Manak Chand approached the Raja of Kangra, Alam Chand, and tried to persuade him to annex the state of Kehloor. However, Alam Chand declined to take any initiative in the absence of permission from the Mughal viceroy at Sirhind. They, therefore, went to Sirhind and, by misrepresentation, induced the viceroy to send his army to invade Bilaspur. However, with the help of Raja of Handur and Ranas of Barah Thakurain, Bhim Chand defeated the enemy at Hatwar. This victory established Bhim Chand in the hills as a powerful warrior. The Raja of Kullu whose territory had been invaded by Bushehr sought help from Bhim Chand, who advanced as far as Nirmand, capturing several forts and expelling the invaders. He restored the lost territory to Kullu. Then, on the request of Raja Mandi, Sidhsen, he invaded Suket and made over Suket territories of Baragarh and Sikander Dhar to Mandi. Feud with Guru Gobind Singh

Raja Bhim Chand, during his reign, was in constant feud2 with the Sikh guru, Gobind Singhji (born on December 22, 1666). Raja Kalyan Chand had earlier gifted two villages of Kiratpur and Kalyanpur to the sixth guru, Hargobind Singh, which continued to be in the possession of Guru Gobind Singh also. At the beginning, the Guruji and Raja Bhim Chand had cordial relations but the ties were strained between the two when the latter requested the guru to send his white elephant that was gifted to him by Raja Ram Rai of Assam. Bhim Chand wanted his son, Ajmer Chand, the would-be groom, to ride this elephant during his marriage with the daughter of Raja Fateh Shah of Garhwal, but the guru refused to accede to the request. Raja Bhim Chand was furious and asked Guru Gobind Singh to vacate the territory of Makhowal (later named as Anand Sahib), which the Raja claimed to have belonged to Bilaspur, failing which, to be ready for a fight. Guru Gobind Singh refuted the claim on the ground that the land in question had actually been purchased. Therefore, it had remained in full ownership of the gurus ever since. When Raja Medini Parkash of Sirmaur heard of the strife between the guru and the Raja of Bilaspur, he readily offered the former all the hospitality and arrangements for permanent stay and settlement in his state. Thereupon, at the advice of Mata Gujri, the guru migrated to Nahan and then came to Paonta Sahib and camped there. This place was fortified in 1685. Men and material further strengthened the garrison. Five hundred Pathan soldiers, earlier retired from Bassi-Pathana, joined the service of the guru. Another detachment of 500 Udasi disciples headed by Mahant Kripal Dass of Ludhiana reached the garrison for military training. Fearing the increasing military might of the guru, Raja Fateh Shah of Garhwal tried to win him over and sent precious presents and invited him to attend the marriage of his daughter. In return, the guru detailed Bhai Nand Chand with 500 soldiers and a detachment of a cavalry to attend the marriage ceremony and also sent gifts to the bride worth Rs. 1.25 lakh. The entourage was received warmly by the Raja and a special campsite for the stay was allotted to them. In the meanwhile, Raja Bhim Chand along with the barat was on the way to Srinagar (Garhwal). Raja Kripal of Kangra, Raja Kesri Chand of Jaswal, Raja Hari Chand of Handur, Raja Prithi Chand of Dhadwal, Raja Sukh Dyal of Jasrot and Rajas of Mandi and Suket accompanied the marriage entourage. A considerable armed force escorted the procession. When Guru Gobind Singh came to know of the arriving barat along with the military force, he sent a force of 500 men and blocked the route of the barat. The other alternate route was too long to reach Srinagar in time. Bhim Chand did not want to undertake a fight at such an auspicious occasion. He, therefore, sent his wazir to meet the guru and requested him to allow the barat to proceed further as the time for the lagan was approaching fast, but the guru refused. Thereupon, the Raja requested Guru Gobind Singh to at least allow the bridegroom and a few more persons to proceed so that the marriage ceremony would take place at the pre-fixed time, the rest of the barat would take another alternate route. To this, the guru finally agreed. When, on arrival at Srinagar, Bhim Chand came to know of the presence of Bhai Nand Chand along with the guru's force stationed at the nearby camp, a letter of expressed protest was sent to Raja Fateh Shah, the bride's father. Fateh Shah also barat and resentment at the guru's action of blocking the route of the an attack returned the presents sent by the guru. Nand Chand sensed on his forces and quietly withdrew from the scene and reported to the guru about the developments. The guru felt humiliated. When the marriage party was returning, a battle between the forces of the guru and Raja Bhim Chand took place in the year 1686 at Bhangani, a village near Paonta Sahib situated between the rivers Yamuna and Giri. As Raja Kripal Chand of Kangra, Raja Kesri Chand Jaswal, Raja Sukhdev Chand Jasrotia, Raja Hari Chand Handuria, Raja Prithi Chand Dadwalia were all accompanying the barat, therefore, they all were engaged in the battle in which, the Rajas of Handur, Jaswal and Jasrot were killed. Guru Gobind Singh has mentioned in his autobiography, Bachitar Natak, the differences with Raja Bhim Chand of Bilaspur. It appears that bitterness between the two was also as a result of interference by the mercenary Pathan soldiers who had joined the guru's army after they had deserted the Mughal forces. These Pathans were repeatedly harassing the hill people. Besides, the hill chiefs did not like the growing military power of the guru in the region and treated this as a threat to their own existence. These hill chiefs were also equally against the continued dominance of the Mughal power.3 Nevertheless, it is clear from historical records that Aurangzeb had exhausted his treasury in the long war with the Marathas waged since 1681. He was in need of money and food. So he ordered all his commanders to immediately collect all the revenues due from the tributaries. Hakim Mian Khan of Lahore sent his commander Alif Khan to collect annual tribute from the Rajas of Kangra, Jaswal, Dhadwal, Jasrot and other neighbouring states. The Raja of Kangra refused to pay any such tribute as the same was being paid to the Raja of Bilaspur under whose suzerainty they had been functioning since the time of Raja Dip Chand. Alif Khan challenged the Raja of Bilaspur and asked him to pay the entire amount of the tribute realized, failing which the state of Kehloor would be taken over. It was on this occasion that Guru Gobind Singh, at the request of Raja Bhim Chand, came to his rescue. A fierce battle followed at Nadaun in 1687, in which Alif Khan was defeated. After the victory, Guru Gobind Singh came to Bilaspur and stayed with the Raja for eight days and paid visits to the local royal temples. He was given a warm reception and rich gifts were offered.4 Later, Alif Khan sent an offer of agreement and reconciliation, which Raja Bhim Chand accepted as the hill chiefs decided to come to terms with the Mughal commander in order to avoid any future likelihood of another Mughal force being sent against them. It was agreed that henceforth, the tributes of eleven states on the other side of the river Ravi would be paid to the Mughal commander at Lahore, whereas eleven states falling this side of the river would continue to remain under Bilaspur and the tributes would be paid to the state of Kehloor. Unfortunately, the agreement was concluded without consulting and associating Guru Gobind Singh, which created further bitterness between the guru and Raja Bhim Chand. However, before long, Bhim Chand patched up and entered into an understanding with the guru and ever after they both started living in peace After Raja Bhim Chand abdicated in favour of his son, Ajmer Chand, in 1692, the latter remained for most of the time at war with Guru Gobind Singh. The new ruler of Bilaspur invaded Sirmaur and built the fort of Ajmergarh on the bank of the Yamuna. In protest, the Raja of Sirmaur lodged a complaint with the viceroy of Sirhind, who invaded Bilaspur. The troops of Bilaspur showered stones over the enemy forces from a higher defensive position and repulsed them. It is interesting to note that Aurangzeb did not approve of the settlement concluded between Alif Khan, Mughal governor of Kangra, and Raja Bhim Chand regarding payment of tribute by twenty-two states. Historian, Bhai Santokh Singh, writes about Guru Gobind and Bilaspur

Bhai Santokh Singh (1785-1845) was born at Taran-Taaran (Punjab). Under the guidance of Gyani Sant Singh, he studied the history of the Sikh gurus, mastered many languages such as Sanskrit, Punjabi, Hindi and Braj. He became a poet of eminence in Braj language. He initially joined the Patiala durbar. Later, Raja of Kethal patronized him as a court poet. By this time he had already authored Namkosh, Guru Nanak Parkash,. Garab Ganjni and translated in Braj the great epic, Valmiki Ramayan. At Kethal, Bhai Santokh Singh undertook of the Sikh gurus the task of authoring the history Pratap in an epic form in Braj language and titled it Sri Guru Department Suraj Granth. The work was completed sometime in 1845. The of Language, Punjab, republished this work in eight volumes in the year 1974-78. This poet-cum-historian has described in detail the ups and downs in the relations of Guru Gobind Singh with the hillchiefs, particularly the Rajas of Kehloor, Bhim Chand and Ajmer Chand who were fighting against the guru most of the time. In the context of Bilaspur (Kehloor), Santokh Singh has written a number of chapters entailing the accounts of Guru Gobind's worshipping of the goddess at Naina Devi, Raja Bhim Chand's feud with the guru alongwith an exclusive reference to Pamma (Nadda), a minister of the Raja of Bilaspur.
