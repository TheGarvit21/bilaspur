Soon after his accession, a revolt had taken place in Handur, which Ganesh Singh Bedi has described in detail in Shasbi Vamsh Vinod (pp.76-77). Raja Man Chand of Nalagarh and his brother, Padam, were involved in a feud. Pamma's son, Gopi Nath, also figured in the affairs of Handur. He wanted to avenge the killing of his father by the Rani of Nalagarh. He was successful in creating a rift between Raja Man Chand of Handur and his brother, Padam. In their fight, both the brothers and their two sons were killed, leaving behind no immediate successor. After consultations, the officials of Handur decided to transfer the reins of the state to the Raja of Kehloor, Devi Chand. Accordingly, people approached Devi Chand and requested him to take over the state. Raja Devi Chand instead agreed that his younger brother, Shakti Chand, could become the Raja of Handur. But the latter declined the offer, as he did not wish to equal his elder brother in status. Devi Chand then placed Gaje Singh on the throne of Handur since he was the nearest relation of the late Raja. Gaje Singh was serving as an official in Bilaspur, his sister was married to Chimna, younger brother of Raja Devi Chand. Henceforth, the Rajas of Handur started suffixing 'Singh' instead of 'Chand' with their names.

Landslide blocked the Sutlej In 1762, a landslide at village Kiyari in Bhajji state blocked Sutlej river for 45 days. Many villages situated near the riverbank were submerged into the water reservoir. Raja Devi Chand assessed the gravity of the situation and placed guards at an interval of every five kos from Bhajji to Ropar. The guards were directed to open fire the moment the dam bursted under the pressure of water. The guards signalled promptly by opening fire from their matchlocks when the dam created by landslide burst. Within half an hour, Ropar was warned. The flood created havoc in lower areas of Bilaspur and the Punjab, but the timely warning system saved a great deal of property and many lives.
